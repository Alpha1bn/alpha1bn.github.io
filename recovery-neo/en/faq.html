Recovery Neo for Android — Help / FAQ

Last updated: October 8, 2026 · Version 1.5.1

1. Which drives and file systems can it recover?

USB sticks, SD and microSD cards (through an OTG adapter or card reader), and USB-C external SSDs or HDDs. FAT32 and exFAT drives are recovered with their original file names and folders. NTFS, ext4, HFS+ and APFS drives are recovered by content carving only: the files come back by type and content, but not their names or folders. The app tells you which mode applies as soon as the drive appears on the home screen. Drives that only speak UAS are not supported yet.

2. Why can't it recover my phone's internal storage?

Android does not give apps raw access to the phone's own storage, so no app can scan it block by block. A microSD card sitting in the phone's slot has the same limitation: take it out and put it in an OTG card reader.

3. What is free and what is paid?

Free: the full ATOLL™ engine 1 scan, file-name recovery, and saving the results after one short rewarded ad. Paid (48-hour pass or Lifetime): no ads, plus ATOLL™ engine 2, which tries to reassemble fragmented photos and unlocks the extra items it finds. Scanning is never gated behind a purchase.

4. 48-hour pass, Lifetime, refunds and restoring purchases

The 48-hour pass starts at the moment of purchase and covers every recovery in that window; buying another pass while one is active adds 48 hours. Lifetime is a one-time purchase. Refunds follow Google Play's policy (typically within 48 hours). Tap "Restore purchases" in the purchase sheet after a reinstall — Lifetime comes back, but a 48-hour pass that was already used cannot be restored. Extra items found by ATOLL 2 may be damaged or incomplete, and a purchase does not guarantee their recovery.

5. The ad doesn't load — can I still save my files?

Yes. If the ad fails twice or you have waited about 20 seconds, the app switches to a short countdown and then saves for free. The same happens offline. "Later" always keeps the recovered files inside the app so you can save them another time.

6. The recovered files look broken or incomplete

Deleted data can be partly overwritten by anything written to the drive afterwards, and such files cannot be made whole. The counts the app shows are verified: 0-byte fragments, duplicates and files that cannot be decoded are excluded and listed under "Excluded as damaged or duplicate". Recovery is never guaranteed — stop using the drive as soon as you notice data is missing.

7. Scanning is slow, the phone gets warm, the battery drains

The scan reads the whole drive once, read-only. Speed is set by the drive and the USB link — around 7 MB/s on USB 2.0 means roughly 70 minutes for 32 GB. A scan is heavy work: the app checks the battery before starting, slows down when the phone gets hot, and keeps the scan alive in a notification while the screen is off. Wireless charging during a scan is fine; the USB-C port is taken by the drive.

8. Can I use Airplane Mode during recovery?

Yes. Scanning, file-system recovery, extraction and the in-app copy all run on the phone with no network. The internet is used only on the results screen (one rewarded ad or a purchase) and at start-up (Google Play update check and ad/billing preparation).

9. Where are the recovered files?

Until you save them, recovered files stay inside the app's private storage — the last three recoveries are kept. "Open results folder" copies them to Downloads/RecoveryNeo/<job>/, a public folder that other apps can read (the app warns you once before the first copy). "Share" sends selected files to another app without copying the whole folder.

10. Privacy — does anything leave my phone?

No. Drive contents and recovered files never leave the phone, and the app has no server. The only network traffic is Google AdMob (rewarded ads and consent), Google Play Billing and Google Play updates. Details are in the Privacy Policy.

11. My drive isn't detected

Use an adapter or cable that is marked OTG / data-capable — a charge-only cable will not work. External SSDs plug straight into USB-C with a data cable. Open the app and tap Refresh; the USB permission dialog appears inside the app the first time it reads the drive (since 1.5.0 Android no longer pops it up while the app is closed). Power-hungry drives may need a self-powered hub. UAS-only drives are not supported yet.

12. How do I contact the developer?

Settings → "Contact developer (email)" opens a message to sungjyny@gmail.com. Please include your phone model, Android version, the drive type and what you saw on screen.
